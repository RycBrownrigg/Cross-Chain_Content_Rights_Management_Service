\chapter{Discussion}\label{chap:discussion}

This chapter analyzes the findings of Chapter~\ref{chap:results}: identifying the questions addressed, the decisions substantiated, and the limitations that shape the interpretation of the contribution.

\section{Answering the Research Questions}\label{sec:answering-rqs}

\subsection{SQ1: Cross-Chain Rights Operations and Finality}\label{subsec:sq1-xcm-rights}

\textbf{Affirmative with one caveat.} XCM v3 is adequate for executing cross-chain rights operations: the canonical sequence \texttt{WithdrawAsset} $\to$ \texttt{BuyExecution} $\to$ \texttt{Transact} manages all five \texttt{xcm\_*} extrinsics with complete atomic success and a finality of 3 to 5 blocks (Section~\ref{sec:technical-results}). No custom XCM extensions are necessary. The only caveat is that cross-chain \textbf{recurring} renewals are contingent upon the readiness of XCM v5 \texttt{Schedule} production; in the meantime, an on-chain block hook manages local auto-renewal.

\subsection[SQ2: Latency versus Decentralization]{SQ2: Latency versus Decentralization Trade-Offs}\label{subsec:sq2-latency-decentralization}

The trade-off is real but highly asymmetric: CCRMS relinquishes approximately five orders of magnitude in latency (see Section~\ref{subsec:comparative-analysis}) in exchange for several orders of magnitude in enhanced decentralization (with HHI approximately 33, compared to 10,000 for a centralized monopoly, as detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:decentralization-hhi}). In contexts where decentralization is decisive (censorship-resistant content, jurisdictionally portable rights, and multi-platform creator economies), the trade-off is advantageous. Conversely, for high-frequency interactive operations, it is less suitable. We do not assert that CCRMS is universally optimal along the latency--decentralization spectrum; rather, we contend that it is appropriate for the discrete-operation use case it targets.

\subsection[SQ3: Unified Rights Token]{SQ3: Unified Rights Token Enforcing All Three Monetization Models}\label{subsec:sq3-unified-rights-token}

\textbf{Yes, and the cost savings are substantial but audience-dependent.} The unified primitive works (Section~\ref{sec:pallet-impl}), the access check resolves rights in priority order across all three storage maps, and royalty propagation applies uniformly to all primary-market revenue. The Monte Carlo simulation (Section~\ref{sec:economic-results}) confirms the cost savings, with the audience-dependent nuance discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:economic-crossover}. The unification itself is the dissertation's most significant \textbf{conceptual} contribution: it closes gap G7 using only standard FRAME primitives, without novel cryptographic or consensus-layer mechanisms.

\subsection{SQ4: Security and Authorization}\label{subsec:sq4-security-authorization}

For purchase operations, the decoupling between payer and beneficiary is economically neutral: no entity can compel another account to make a payment without that account's signature, so \texttt{ensure\_signed} is sufficient. In contrast, the situation with \texttt{xcm\_transfer\_ownership} differs: the extrinsic accepts a \texttt{from} parameter, and the initial implementation failed to verify whether the caller was related to that account. The findings from B's security audit addressed this issue through a simple, one-line check (Section~\ref{subsec:security-audit}). The overarching lesson is that \textbf{cross-chain extrinsics that accept identity parameters require explicit authorization checks}, even when the same operation in a local context would not require such verification.

\subsection[SQ5: Economic Viability]{SQ5: Economic Viability under Realistic Creator Audience Distributions}\label{subsec:sq5-economic-viability}

\textbf{Yes, conditionally.} The Monte Carlo simulation (Section~\ref{sec:economic-results}) provides a definitive empirical response: CCRMS yields higher mean creator revenue across all four simulated platform configurations, exhibiting an advantage ranging from 8.9\% to 16.9\%. However, this advantage is heavily dependent on the size of the creator's audience. We explore the crossover point and its implications in Section~\ref{subsec:economic-crossover}. Our contribution lies in transforming this conditionality into a \textbf{quantitative and reproducible} framework rather than merely qualitative observations.

\section{Interpretation of Key Results}\label{sec:interpretation}

\subsection{The 26.2 TPS Figure in Context}\label{subsec:26-tps-context}

The figure of 26.2 TPS reflects a single parachain operating on a pre-Polkadot~2.0 toolchain and does not represent the architecture's maximum capacity. Polkadot achieves scalability through \textbf{horizontal}: with 100 parachains at 26 TPS each, the total capacity reaches 2,600 TPS; additionally, Elastic Scaling (currently in production) enhances per-parachain throughput across multiple cores. More importantly, the $270\times$ ratio relative to the centralized baseline is misleading. CCRMS is designed to process low-frequency events; it records discrete rights transactions with cryptographic finality, automatic royalty propagation, and cross-chain portability, none of which the centralized baseline provides at any throughput level. When assessed against its actual workload, 26.2 TPS comfortably surpasses the demands of every creator-economy platform.

\subsection{The Economic Crossover at $\sim$2,000 Followers}\label{subsec:economic-crossover}

The economic advantage of CCRMS predominantly benefits creators with established audiences. For those with fewer than $\sim$2,000 followers, centralized platforms tend to generate greater revenue, as algorithmic discovery facilitates access to an audience that the creator might not otherwise reach. While this initially appears to challenge the notion that CCRMS serves as a creator-friendly alternative, the more accurate and credible assertion is the conditional: (decentralized = better for creators \textbf{with established audiences}). CCRMS is primarily a tool designed \textbf{for creators who have surpassed the discovery benefits of centralized platforms and seek to maximize revenue retention}. This perspective aligns with the Web3 product literature, which has consistently observed that early adopters of crypto-economic platforms are users for whom alternative options are no longer optimal.

\subsection{The Snowbridge End-to-End Demonstration}\label{subsec:snowbridge-demonstration}

The CCRMS parachain served as a \textbf{passive participant} in the Snowbridge test: the runtime XCM configuration was pre-arranged to receive Ethereum-originated messages without requiring modification. The integration succeeded because we had configured the runtime for it early, at minimal cost. Our development against V1 demonstrates forward compatibility with the production V2 bridge (Section~\ref{sec:work-in-context}). The final hop (bridged ETH paying for a CCRMS operation) was not executed end-to-end (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); a production deployment would address this deficiency.

\section{Architectural Decisions Revisited}\label{sec:architectural-decisions-revisited}

Were the major architectural decisions vindicated in light of the measurements and the events of the implementation window?

\textbf{The pallet-first decision is vindicated most emphatically.} Appendix~\ref{sec:ink-discontinuation} documents how the discontinuation of ink! in January 2026 substantively confirmed a decision we had previously made for unrelated engineering reasons. The overarching lesson: \textbf{architectural decisions that reduce reliance on any single non-essential component yield benefits when ecosystem-level changes take place.} This principle is consistently reflected in our use of \texttt{pallet-nfts} rather than the discontinued RMRK pallets, HRMP rather than custom inter-parachain protocols, and the avoidance of dependencies on any single relayer.

\textbf{The RMRK 2.0 to \texttt{pallet-nfts} pivot is vindicated} (Appendix~\ref{sec:rmrk-to-pallet-nfts}). The RMRK pallets remain frozen and dormant; any dissertation that depended on them would now encounter the same compatibility issues previously faced. The task to reproduce the required semantics proved to be less extensive than anticipated: a few hundred lines of Rust built upon a maintained foundation. \textbf{Reimplementation of standards is sometimes more advantageous than adoption of standards} when the reference implementation is unmaintained.

\textbf{The unified rights token model is vindicated with a positioning caveat.} The design works correctly, scales linearly, and imposes no measurable performance overhead. The caveat is that unification matters more for creators seeking to offer multiple monetization models simultaneously than for those who use only a single model. We claim the unification holds broad value; but the empirical evaluation does not directly substantiate this claim. We recognize this as an avenue for future research, as outlined in Section~\ref{subsec:audience-validation-future}.

\textbf{The cross-chain operation pattern is vindicated with a caveat.} The decoupling of payer and beneficiary functions effectively operates for purchase transactions, where the asymmetry holds no significant economic impact. However, an explicit authorization verification for \texttt{xcm\_transfer\_ownership} is necessary (Finding~B). The cross-chain extrinsic pattern is generally considered \textbf{primarily secure}, yet it presents at least one critical aspect that necessitates careful scrutiny. Future systems adopting this pattern should anticipate the need for thorough auditing of these critical points.

\section{The Work in Context}\label{sec:work-in-context}

The Polkadot ecosystem evolved during implementation. \textbf{Polkadot~2.0} features (Async Backing, Agile Coretime, Elastic Scaling) reached production by late 2025, indicating our latency metrics are upper bounds for pre-2.0 testing; deployment uses the updated SDK with no pallet-level changes. \textbf{Snowbridge V2} launched in November 2025, with \$75M TVL and 50\% fee reduction; our V1 shows compatibility with new features. \textbf{Story Protocol} (Feb 2025, \$140M funding) confirms market premise and compares: single-chain Layer~1 vs.\ cross-chain parachain. \textbf{EU regulation} (AI Act copyright Aug 2025, MiCA July 2026) drives demand for cryptographic provenance and royalty infrastructure via CCRMS; subscription tokens under MiCA remain legally unclear. Recent studies \citep{madapatiPradhan2025, dengEtAl2025, liEtAl2025a, maricEtAl2025} improve context but do not change conclusions.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The CCRMS prototype has five principal limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No production deployment.} All measurements come from a local Zombienet topology with two relay-chain validators, single-collator parachains, and no real-world latency. Chapter~\ref{chap:results} results serve as upper bounds in this test environment and do not predict production behavior. In production, latency might improve with Polkadot~2.0, throughput could stay similar, and reliability could be diminished under real-world conditions.
\item \textbf{Single-author validation.} A single author performed the implementation, test design, measurement collection, and interpretation. No independent code review, third-party security audit, or external benchmark validation has been conducted. Production deployment would require all three before any non-trivial value passes through the system.
\item \textbf{Local-only Snowbridge integration.} Geth and Lodestar ran on the same machine as the Polkadot side, avoiding many realities of public Ethereum (variable block times, MEV, network contention, beacon chain reorgs). The integration serves as a proof of architectural feasibility but provides no data on production bridge behavior.
\item \textbf{The 50-child cap.} The \texttt{BoundedVec<\_, ConstU32<50>{}>} limit on \texttt{Children} is the most pressing operational constraint. Popular content items will hit this limit; we identify three mitigation paths in Section~\ref{subsec:bounded-vec-future}, but none have been implemented in the prototype.
\item \textbf{No empirical proof of the bridged-payment final hop.} The bridged-ETH-pays-for-CCRMS-operation flow is conceptually straightforward but has not been exercised end-to-end. We claim forward-compatibility with the flow, which is a weaker claim than an empirical demonstration would be.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implications}\label{sec:implications}

The findings extend beyond the immediate prototype in four directions.

\textbf{For platform designers,} the most actionable lesson is that \textbf{architectural choices that minimize dependence on any single non-essential component pay dividends when ecosystem-level changes occur}; the pallet-first decision was made for unrelated engineering reasons but provided incidental robustness against the ink! discontinuation. Two secondary lessons: \textbf{cross-chain extrinsic patterns require explicit authorization audits at the corners} (Finding~B), and \textbf{standards reimplementation is sometimes preferable to standards adoption} when the reference implementation is unmaintained (the RMRK 2.0 pivot).

\textbf{For creators,} the Monte Carlo simulation indicates that ``is a decentralized platform actually better for me?'' with a conditional affirmative response: \textbf{yes, depending on the audience size}. Creators with over 10,000 followers substantially benefit from reduced fees and direct revenue retention; conversely, those with fewer than $\sim$2,000 followers are typically better served by centralized platforms, whose algorithmic discovery delivers an audience they could not otherwise reach. The choice should be based on the creator's career stage rather than a universal preference. Additionally, there is an operational benefit to simplifying multi-model unification: a creator managing separate Patreon and Gumroad accounts for identical content can, through CCRMS, register the content once and offer all three models from a single record.

\textbf{For regulators,} the EU AI Act and MiCA (Section~\ref{sec:work-in-context}) create demand for systems that can prove content provenance and record rights and royalty distributions in tamper-evident form. CCRMS provides exactly these properties, and on-chain rights management systems should be considered as \textbf{compliance infrastructure} for emerging copyright and AI training regimes rather than as alternative payment platforms. The open question regarding MiCA's classification of subscription tokens with fungible characteristics would benefit from regulatory clarity, because the current ambiguity poses risk for would-be EU deployers.

\textbf{For the research community,} the prototype, implementation specifications, and Monte Carlo simulation are all positioned for follow-on research. The methodological contribution is a worked DSR example for multi-component blockchain prototyping. The empirical contribution is the audience-dependent finding, which future research could refine using real-world migration data or models that incorporate creator psychology and switching costs.

\section{Critical Self-Reflection}\label{sec:self-reflection}

In retrospect, we would have \textbf{initiated the Snowbridge integration at an earlier stage}; the complexity of setup, pinning the Lodestar version, interpreting forked versions, and managing the beacon state cache timing collectively consumed several days of unforeseen debugging. Additionally, we would have \textbf{prioritized the FRAME benchmarking} earlier to enable reporting throughput and latency as fee-adjusted figures rather than merely raw measurements.

We would \textbf{not} change the pallet-first decision, the unified rights token model, or the cross-chain operation pattern. These are the most consequential decisions in the system and have aged best.

\section{Summary}\label{sec:chapter-8-summary}

The five research sub-questions are answered in the affirmative, with the most economically significant finding being the audience-dependent advantage of CCRMS. The major architectural decisions are vindicated to varying degrees, most emphatically the pallet-first decision, as evidenced by the January 2026 ink! discontinuation. The five principal limitations constrain how the contribution should be read, but do not invalidate the principal findings. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} presents the conclusion and future work.
