\begin{abstract}

The content creator economy concentrates value in centralized intermediaries; no existing system delivers subscription, pay-per-view, and permanent-ownership access rights through a single cross-chain-portable primitive. This dissertation asks: \textit{how can a Polkadot parachain built on FRAME pallet primitives and XCM deliver a unified rights token natively supporting all three monetization models across heterogeneous blockchain networks, and what levels of transaction finality, cost efficiency, and creator revenue retention can such a system achieve?}

CCRMS (Cross-Chain Content Rights Management Service) is a Polkadot parachain implementing parent--child NFT nesting on \texttt{pallet-nfts}, automatic royalty splits, and cross-chain portability via XCM and Snowbridge. The prototype is evaluated with 56 unit tests, seven performance benchmarks, a security audit, a centralized Express.js/SQLite baseline comparison, and a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo creator revenue simulation.

All 11 KPIs met their targets: 26.2~TPS sustained throughput, $\sim$6-second inclusion latency, 3--5-block cross-chain finality, 191 bytes per content item, 90\% baseline uptime, and 15--20-second MTTR. The Monte Carlo simulation yields $\sim$16.8\% higher average creator revenue than centralized platforms, but this advantage is \textbf{strongly audience-dependent}: creators above $\sim$10,000 followers benefit decisively, while those below $\sim$2,000 are better served by centralized discovery platforms.

Three findings advance the state of knowledge. First, a unified rights token supporting all three access models can be constructed from standard FRAME primitives without novel cryptographic mechanisms, closing literature gap G7. Second, the revenue advantage of decentralized rights management is not universal, crossing a follower threshold at approximately 2,000. Third, the work demonstrates that conservatively chosen architectural decisions---specifically the pallet-first design---provide incidental resilience against ecosystem-level changes, as evidenced by the January 2026 discontinuation of ink!\ development.

\end{abstract}
