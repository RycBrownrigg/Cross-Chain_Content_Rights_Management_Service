\chapter{Results}\label{chap:results}

This chapter presents the measured results. Section~\ref{sec:technical-results} covers technical results, Section~\ref{sec:economic-results} covers economic results, and Section~\ref{sec:security-user-results} covers security and user-centric results. \section{Technical Results}\label{sec:technical-results}

The technical results in this section address SQ1 (XCM cross-chain finality, Section~\ref{subsec:latency}), SQ2 (the latency--decentralization trade-off, Section~\ref{subsec:comparative-analysis}), and SQ3 (unified rights token throughput and storage performance, Sections~\ref{subsec:throughput}--\ref{subsec:storage}).

\subsection{Throughput}\label{subsec:throughput}

We evaluated all 11 local extrinsic types across four batch sizes (1, 10, 50, 100). At a batch size of 100, extrinsics without nesting constraints achieved 16.6 TPS, whereas those involving the minting of child NFTs (\texttt{subscribe}, \texttt{purchase\_ownership}, \texttt{enable\_auto\_renew}, \texttt{disable\_auto\_renew}, \texttt{transfer\_ownership}) were limited to 8.3 TPS by the maximum children constraint (\texttt{MaxChildren=50}) rather than by block weight. The CCRMS rights management extrinsics matched the baseline throughput, confirming that the unified rights token model incurs no measurable performance overhead.

\medskip
TPS scales linearly with batch size, given a 6-second block time and one block per batch. During the sustained stress test, 200 concurrent accounts submitted \texttt{register\_content} transactions over 180 seconds, achieving a sustained throughput of \textbf{26.2 TPS} (174.6 transactions per block across 27 blocks). Block weight utilization at 300 transactions was only \textbf{12.9\% of \texttt{ref\_time}} and \textbf{2.2\% of \texttt{proof\_size}}, indicating an estimated theoretical maximum of approximately \textbf{283 TPS} per parachain. The stress test had a transaction failure rate of 78\%, primarily due to RPC-layer nonce conflicts rather than parachain failures; all successfully included transactions were processed accurately.

This throughput is therefore sufficient for the use case without approaching architectural limits.

\subsection{Test Coverage}\label{subsec:test-coverage}

Our unit test suite covers \textbf{16 of the 19 distinct error variants} defined in \texttt{pallet-content-rights}, achieving 84 percent coverage, which exceeds the Phase~2 objective of 80 percent. Three variants remain untested and are out of scope for the unit test suite: \texttt{ItemIdOverflow} requires exhausting a \texttt{u32} counter under normal extrinsic dispatch, which is not feasible in a test environment; \texttt{NftOperationFailed} is triggered only by internal \texttt{pallet-nfts} state corruption that the mock runtime does not replicate; and \texttt{ChildAlreadyNested} cannot be reached through any publicly exposed extrinsic. The comprehensive error variant table is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:security}.

\subsection{Static Analysis and Dependency Audit}\label{subsec:static-analysis}

Static analysis with \texttt{cargo clippy} reported zero warnings in the dissertation's codebase. \texttt{cargo audit} reported 8 advisories, all related to transitive dependencies of the Polkadot SDK, including networking, the WebAssembly (WASM) executor, Transport Layer Security (TLS), and logging. Importantly, none of these advisories affect our pallet code. These issues are effectively mitigated by the architectural separation between the sandboxed WASM runtime and the node binary. See the Appendix~\ref{app:security} for the full advisory breakdown.
